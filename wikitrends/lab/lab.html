Play with the evidence

Six hands-on views of the data behind the report. Drag, tap and shuffle the data itself; the pre-registered result stays on screen as a dashed outline.

1Shadow bars 2Shuffle the labels 3Pull the loudest out 4Definition flipbook 5Word chips 6Language prism 7Galaxy
1

Shadow bars

Exploratory

Bar: how much loaded words (colonial, apartheid, genocide…) rose in each kind of topic article, per 10,000 words. Grey shadow: how much the comparison articles rose. The coloured part sticking out is the extra change.

2

Shuffle the labels

Exploratory

If the topic label meant nothing, random labels would give an extra change as big as the real one. Shuffle and see how often chance does as well.

3

Pull the loudest out

Exploratory

Is the extra change carried by a few extreme articles? Each dot is one article. Drag a handle inwards to drop the extremes.

4

Definition flipbook

Exploratory

The first sentence is the definition most readers see. Pick an article, then swipe the sentence to flip through its versions.

5

Word chips

Exploratory

Tap a chip, or drag it onto the chart, to draw those words per 10,000 words. Tap again or flick it off to remove.

6

Language prism

Exploratory

The same articles in other Wikipedias. Each cell: change in loaded words in the opening paragraph, per 10,000 words. Tap a language to sort. Drag EN onto another language to subtract.

7

Galaxy

Exploratory

Every dot is one article, about ten thousand in all, grouped by WikiProject. Across: change in loaded words since the first snapshot, per 10,000 words. Pick a date to watch them move; tap a row to compare.
