Data lab Exploratory

Play with the evidence

Eight hands-on views of the same data behind the report. Drag, tap and shuffle the data itself. The pre-registered result stays on screen as a faint outline, so you can always see where you started.

1

Shadow bars

Exploratory

Each bar is one kind of topic article. Its length is how much the loaded words (colonial, apartheid, genocide and so on) rose per 10,000 words. The grey shadow behind it is how much the comparison articles rose over the same time. Only the coloured part sticks out past what happened anyway.

2

Shuffle the labels

Exploratory

If the topic label meant nothing, you could hand it to any articles at random and get gaps as big as the real one. Shuffle the labels and see how often chance does as well.

3

Pull the loudest out

Exploratory

Is the gap carried by a few extreme articles? Each dot is one article’s change. Drag a handle inwards to drop the most extreme articles and watch the gap respond.

4

Definition flipbook

Exploratory

The first sentence is the definition most readers see. Pick an article, then swipe the sentence left or right to flip through every version editors saved.

5

Word chips

Exploratory

Drag a chip onto the chart, or tap it, to draw how often those words appear. Flick a chip off the chart to remove it. Press and hold a chip to break it into single words.

6

Lead vs body

Exploratory

Did the opening paragraph change more than the rest of the article? One dot per article. Across: change in loaded words in the full text. Up: change in the opening paragraph. Above the diagonal, the opening rose more.

7

Language prism

Exploratory

The same articles in other Wikipedias: same rules and software, different communities. Each cell is how much the loaded words in that language’s opening changed. Tap a language to sort by it. Drag English onto another language to see English minus that language.

8

Galaxy

Exploratory

Every dot is one article, grouped by the WikiProject that rates it, with about ten thousand in all. Across is how much its loaded words changed since the first snapshot. Pick a date and watch them move. Tap a project to compare it with the comparison projects.
