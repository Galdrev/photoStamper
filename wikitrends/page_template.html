Wikipedia Framing Drift  ·  a revision studyEnglish Wikipedia · 2021–2026

Did Wikipedia’s articles on Jews, Israel and Zionism drift toward an anti-colonial framing?

A revision-by-revision measurement of English Wikipedia articles on Judaism, Israel, Zionism, antisemitism and the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, set against articles on other nations, conflicts and religions.

The test

Change in the topic articles, minus change in the comparison articles

This is the pre-registered difference-in-differences. Each row compares the groups’ mean change in one frame over the chosen window. The bar is a bootstrap 95% interval. A filled point means that the result passes a permutation test after correcting for the number of tests (Benjamini–Hochberg q < 0.05). A hollow point means the result is not distinguishable from zero. The three primary outcomes, P1 to P3, were fixed before the data arrived.

Robustness: do the primary results survive other reasonable choices?

A check holds when the estimate keeps the sign of the main result and stays significant (p < 0.05). The placebo vocabulary check is the reverse: it holds when geography and climate words show no group difference. R3 compares the post-war yearly gap with every pre-war year.

Checking the instrument

Does the counter see changes that are known to have happened?

Press coverage documented several specific, dated edits. If the measurement works, the lead or text should lack the wording before the date and contain it after. A second check codes a sample of sentences blind to the article and date.

Method and limits

How this was measured

Data
Every revision since 1 January 2021 from the Wikipedia API, a full-text snapshot of each article on the first day of every quarter, talk-page activity and monthly pageviews. Collection ran on GitHub Actions.
Groups
Topic articles are hand-picked plus a random sample from Zionism, antisemitism, conflict and Jewish-history categories. Comparison articles cover other nationalisms, states, conflicts, genocides and religions. Comparison articles closely tied to the conflict are reported but excluded from the tests.
Text
References, templates, tables, files and back matter are stripped before counting, so citation titles do not add to the counts. The lead is everything before the first section heading. Snapshots under 300 words (40 for the lead) are not measured.
Test
Difference-in-differences on the balanced panel of articles that existed in January 2021: each article’s change in rate, averaged by group, topic minus comparison. A permutation test gives p (5,000 shuffles), with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within the primary and the secondary family. 2,000 bootstrap resamples give the interval.
Limits
Counting words measures emphasis, not accuracy. “Genocide” can grow because a court case exists, not because of bias. Lexicons miss paraphrase and sentence-level stance. Category-sampled articles vary in quality. Editors are reported only in aggregate, and no claim is made about any editor’s intent.
The lexiconPatterns are case-insensitive regular expressions. Exclusions (in amber) are subtracted.
