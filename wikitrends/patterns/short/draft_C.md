# The short answer

## Bottom line

Partly: after October 2023, a few hundred English Wikipedia articles on Israel and Palestine, including some of the most read, took on more of the language of genocide, ethnic cleansing and expulsion, much of it retelling 1948, while Judaism articles did not change and the wave has passed.

## Key points

**1. Few articles changed, but they are the ones people read.**
Articles whose opening paragraph gained critical words get 15% of topic reading, well above the comparison share.
*Visual:* two bars, share of articles vs share of reading.

**2. The story moved back to 1948 and the land.**
Most of the extra change, 89%, is in sentences that are not about recent events.
*Visual:* the Nakba opening line. 2021: "the destruction of Palestinian society and homeland in 1948". 2026: "the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists".

**3. Israel and Palestine pages moved. Judaism pages did not.**
The rise held up in the sealed half of the data. Judaism articles showed no change.
*Visual:* one bar per subject, Judaism at zero.

**4. It came as one wave, and it is over.**
By 2026 the rate of new critical words was back at or below its pre-war level.
*Visual:* bars by half-year, with 7 October 2023 marked.

**5. Experienced regulars wrote it, not newcomers.**
Low-activity accounts made about half the share of edits they made on comparison pages.
*Visual:* two bars, topic vs comparison.

## How we checked

- We compared 4,642 topic articles with 5,678 comparison articles.
- The rise held up on the sealed half. A test fixed in advance, averaged over all articles, found no clear rise.
- We set aside sentences about recent events. Most of the change remained.
- We dropped claims that failed checks, such as how 1948 departures are described.

## What this does not show

- It does not show bias, or whether any wording is true.
- It does not show intent or coordination. We cannot see off-Wikipedia activity.
- It does not show what readers came to believe.

## Common questions

**So is Wikipedia biased?** This study cannot say. It shows which story is now the baseline, not whether it is right.

**Who did this?** Established editors, mostly on quiet pages where nobody undid it. Later-blocked accounts did no more than their usual share.

**Is it just because of the war?** The war brought the editors. Court rulings did not. Editors then mostly rewrote older history.

**Did it only happen in English?** English and Portuguese moved. Nine other languages showed no clear change.

**Does anyone read it?** Yes, though most reading lands on unchanged pages. Search engines and AI tools quote these paragraphs.

## Glossary

- **Opening paragraph:** the summary at the top of an article.
- **Comparison articles:** articles on other countries and conflicts, checked the same way.
- **Critical words:** genocide, ethnic cleansing, expulsion, occupation, apartheid and similar.
- **Sealed half:** data kept unopened until the test was fixed.
