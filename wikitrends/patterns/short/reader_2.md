# Reader 2 (skeptical professional, alert to slant either way). Order read: B, C, A

## Draft B (shop window)

**Claim (2 sentences).** On average Wikipedia's coverage didn't move, but a few hundred heavily read Israel/Palestine pages now open with a different baseline story (1948, the land, "genocide", "ethnic cleansing"). That is mostly older history rather than war news, it came as one wave that has passed, and Judaism pages are untouched.

**Quiz**
- Q1: Barely on average. 3.9% of articles gained critical words in the opening paragraph, but those carry 15% of reading.
- Q2: Israel/Palestine/Zionism changed, Judaism did not. Quiet pages changed more than edit-war pages.
- Q3: Mostly not war news (89% remains after removing recent-event sentences).
- Q4: One wave through 2024, back to pre-war pace by 2026. Not still happening.
- Q5: No. It says explicitly that it proves neither bias, intent nor coordination.

**Slant**
- "the front window ... has been redressed" implies deliberate staging. "Redressed" is not neutral.
- "So before you quote that first sentence, step inside." and "The store is the same. The window is not." This is advice and a verdict, not a finding.
- "Lopsided, not one-sided." This is an editorial judgment.
- "genocide and ethnic cleansing" in the one-line answer, with no attribution and no mention of other vocabulary.
- The Zionism 2021 vs 2026 quote pair is a selective example that implies the change is the story. "through colonization" is the loaded phrase.
- "Who did this? Established editors" casts it as a whodunit.
- Implied but not shown: that the "baseline story" is now one-sided. The antisemitism counter-example (37 to 54) is a single line.

**Confusion.** "critical words" (critical of whom? never defined), "topic articles", "At the same level of editing", "kept sealed until the end", "baseline story", "Quiet pages changed most".

**Ratings.** Clarity 7, fairness 5, memorability 9, trust 6.

## Draft C (plain bullets)

**Claim.** After Oct 2023 a few hundred English Wikipedia Israel/Palestine articles, including heavily read ones, took on more genocide/ethnic cleansing/expulsion language, mostly retelling 1948. Judaism pages didn't change, the wave is over, and it doesn't show bias, intent or what readers believe.

**Quiz**
- Q1: A few hundred articles. The 15% of reading is given, but the share of articles is never stated ("well above the comparison share" has no number). The draft didn't make that clear.
- Q2: Israel/Palestine changed, Judaism did not.
- Q3: Mostly 1948 history (89%).
- Q4: After Oct 2023, one wave, over by 2026.
- Q5: No.

**Slant**
- "took on more of the language of genocide" in the lead.
- The Nakba pair: "the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists" is stark, and the 2021 version is itself not neutral.
- "Experienced regulars wrote it" and "Later-blocked accounts did no more than their usual share" hint at a sockpuppet theory.
- The glossary lists only one-directional words ("occupation, apartheid"). Nothing says the opposite vocabulary was measured, so it implies the other side was not examined.
- "It came as one wave, and it is over." is very confident.

**Confusion.** "The rise held up in the sealed half" vs "A test fixed in advance ... found no clear rise" (seems contradictory), "Low-activity accounts made about half the share", "well above the comparison share", "sealed half".

**Ratings.** Clarity 6, fairness 6, memorability 5, trust 6.

## Draft A (front door, full)

**Claim.** Most articles didn't change, but a few hundred heavily read Israel/Palestine pages (after Oct 2023) now open with a 1948 and land-centred story and words of accusation, while Judaism pages didn't move. The wave peaked in 2024 and ended, the wording stayed, and it can't say who is right.

**Quiz**
- Q1: 85% of articles unchanged. The top 1% (40 pages) supplied 38% of added words. Changed pages are 3.9% of articles and 15% of views.
- Q2: Israel and Palestine pages up, Judaism flat (28% of articles, 7% of additions).
- Q3: No. 89% of the extra change is in sentences not about recent events.
- Q4: Began after Oct 2023, peaked 2024, back to the pre-war rate by 2026. The wording remains.
- Q5: No. It explicitly says not intent or coordination, and it notes that some changes ran the other way.

**Slant**
- "words of accusation" and "loaded words" are the draft's own judgmental labels. They imply the wording is a charge, not description.
- The "front door" metaphor.
- The quote "Israel began committing genocide against Palestinians in Gaza" is stated in Wikipedia's own voice and is the most inflammatory line in all three drafts. Without the attribution context it overstates.
- The Nakba quote pair.
- "Experienced regulars ... not a flood of new or banned accounts" and "an early claim about banned accounts" imply a theory the reader isn't told about.
- "Some changes ran the other way, and most new labels arrived attributed" is the fairest sentence, but it is unquantified and sits at the bottom. It undercuts the headline without evidence.
- The 30-second version ends with the one most-quoted line and "the front door changed", which leaves an emphasis of its own.

**Confusion.** "Lorenz curve", "statistically the same as zero", "+2.22 per 10,000 words", "10,320 articles" vs "4,642 topic articles" (not in this draft, but the numbers differ across drafts), "per 1,000 edits". The visual specs are mixed into the prose, so it reads like a design brief.

**Ratings.** Clarity 6, fairness 7, memorability 6, trust 8.

## Verdict

Most trustworthy for a mixed audience: **Draft A**, narrowly. It has the most numbers, attributes the key quote to a year, says changes ran both ways, says most labels were attributed, and names the failed results. Draft B is the most memorable, but its metaphor and closing line are an editorial. Draft C is cleanest, but it lacks numbers and has a one-way glossary.

**Must-fix edits for A before public use**
1. Replace "words of accusation" and "loaded words" with a neutral defined term ("contested terms such as genocide, ethnic cleansing, apartheid"). Define it once in plain English.
2. Add the measurement of opposite-direction language (antisemitism, terrorism, etc.) as a sentence in the main body, with numbers. Move "some changes ran the other way, most labels attributed" up and quantify it.
3. Drop or heavily caveat the "Israel began committing genocide" quote, or show the Wikipedia attribution and that it is a one-sentence example. Show an example from the other direction as a pair.
4. Replace "front door" in the headline, or add one line saying it is a metaphor for prominence, not for design or intent.
5. State what the 2021 baseline already contained ("destruction of Palestinian society" was not neutral either).
6. Cut jargon (Lorenz, statistically zero, per 10,000 words). State "x of y" plainly.
7. Explain the "banned accounts" and "experienced regulars" lines or delete them, so they don't imply sockpuppetry.
8. Fix the contradiction in the sealed-half line: explain that the average test found nothing but the targeted test held up.
9. Remove "wave is over" certainty or add "as of 2026 data".
