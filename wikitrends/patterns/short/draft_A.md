# The average didn't move. The front door did.

**The short answer:** Partly. Most articles did not change, but a few hundred Israel and Palestine pages, including some of the most-read, now open with a baseline story centred on 1948, the land and words of accusation; Judaism articles did not move.

## What happened

We tracked 10,320 English Wikipedia articles on Israel, Palestine, Zionism and Judaism from 2021 to 2026, against similar articles on other conflicts. After October 2023, editors added words like genocide, ethnic cleansing and expulsion to the opening paragraphs of a few heavily read pages. The wave peaked in 2024 and has ended; the new wording remains.

## Five things that matter

### 1. Most articles did not change, and articles on Judaism did not move at all.
85% of topic articles ended with exactly the same count of these words.

**Visual:** A curve showing how concentrated the change is (a Lorenz curve), four lines: topic before and after the war, comparison before and after. Label two points: the top 1% of articles (40 pages) supplied 38% of the added words; the top 5% supplied 78%. Beside it, a small bar strip by project: Israel and Palestine pages up (together 61% of articles, 84% of additions), Judaism pages flat (28% of articles, 7% of additions; difference from comparison articles −0.1 points, statistically the same as zero).

### 2. The pages that changed are the ones people read.
The few that changed carry 15% of all reading on the topic.

**Visual:** Paired bars, "share of articles" next to "share of views". Topic: 3.9% of articles, 15% of views. Comparison: 1.5% of articles, 5% of views. A thin grey bar underneath reads "83% of topic views still land on opening paragraphs that did not change". Annotate the biggest pages with last-12-month views: Benjamin Netanyahu 7.0M, Israel 4.6M, Palestine 2.4M, Zionism 1.8M, Hamas 1.3M.

**Quote (Israel, 2026):** "After the Hamas-led October 7 attacks, Israel began committing genocide against Palestinians in Gaza."

### 3. The story moved back to 1948 and to the land, not to the war.
89% of the extra change is in sentences not about recent events.

**Visual:** A then/now card for the Nakba article, the two first sentences stacked, changed words highlighted. Below it, one stacked bar: total change in topic opening paragraphs compared with similar articles (+2.22 per 10,000 words), split into older history and land (+1.98) and recent events (+0.24).

**Quote (Nakba):** 2021: "The Nakba ... was the destruction of Palestinian society and homeland in 1948 ..." 2026: "The Nakba ... is the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists ..."

### 4. It came as one wave, and the wave is over.
New loaded words per 1,000 edits peaked at about 16 in 2024, then fell back to the pre-war level.

**Visual:** Half-year bars of new loaded words per 1,000 topic edits: about 7 from 2021 to mid-2023 (range 5.3 to 7.7), 13.5 (late 2023), 16.0 (early 2024), 15.9 (late 2024), 10.8 and 8.2 (2025), 5.1 and 5.9 (2026). Dashed line at the pre-war level. Vertical marker at 7 October 2023. A small note: comparison articles fell over the same period.

### 5. Experienced regulars wrote it, mostly on quiet pages.
Newer accounts made about half the share of edits they made on comparison pages.

**Visual:** Two grouped bars, topic vs comparison: newer accounts 9.1% vs 17.2%. Second panel, "did banned accounts do more than their share?": 13.7% of additions by now-banned accounts against a normal 12.3% on the same pages, drawn as two nearly equal bars. Third panel: topic pages with few reverts changed 28.5% of the time, contested pages about 13%.

## Why you can trust this

Every result is compared with 5,678 similar articles on other conflicts, so normal Wikipedia churn does not count. The main result held on half of the data we kept sealed until the end. Removing sentences about recent events left most of the change in place, so this is not just war news. We dropped our own weak results, including one on how 1948 departures are described and an early claim about banned accounts.

## What this does not mean

- Not that the new wording is true or false. Counting words measures emphasis.
- Not intent or coordination. We found no sign of organised teams, but cannot see off-wiki planning.
- Not that all of Wikipedia shifted. Some changes ran the other way, and most new labels arrived attributed ("according to").

## Questions people ask

**So is Wikipedia biased?** The data can't say. It shows which story is now stated, not whether it is right.

**Who did this?** Experienced regulars, not a flood of new or banned accounts.

**Is it just the war?** Battles brought editors in; court rulings did not. But they mostly wrote about 1948.

**Only in English?** English and Portuguese moved; nine other languages did not detectably.

**Does anyone read it?** Yes. Search engines and AI assistants quote these opening sentences.

## The 30-second version

We tracked ten thousand Wikipedia articles over five years. On average, almost nothing changed. But a few hundred Israel and Palestine pages, including some of the most-read, now open differently. The Nakba is now "the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists." The new wording is mostly about 1948, not the war. It came in one wave in 2024, from experienced editors. Judaism articles did not move. We can't say who is right. We can say the front door changed.
