# Most articles did not change; the opening paragraphs of a few hundred widely read Israel and Palestine articles did

**Summary.** We compared 4,642 English Wikipedia articles on Israel, Palestine, Zionism and Judaism with about 5,700 comparison articles, 2021 to 2026. Most topic articles did not change on our measure, and Judaism articles changed no more than comparison articles. The change we found is concentrated in the opening paragraphs of a few hundred Israel and Palestine articles, several heavily read, and mostly concerns 1948 and the land, not the current war. It came mainly in 2024, had returned to the pre-war rate by 2026, and did not run in only one direction.

*Critical vocabulary:* words from the legal and historical charges made against Israel, such as genocide, apartheid, ethnic cleansing, occupation, colonial, Nakba/expulsion.

## Key findings

**1. Most articles did not change, and Judaism articles did not move.** 85% of topic articles ended with the same count of critical vocabulary. Judaism versus comparison: −0.1 percentage points (interval −2.3 to +2.3), statistically equivalent to zero.
*See the chart: scrolly vocabulary chart.*

**2. The changed articles get more reading than their number suggests.** 3.9% of topic articles gained critical vocabulary in their opening paragraph; they get 15% of topic views (interval 9.5% to 21.3%). Comparison articles: 1.5% and 5%. An extra tilt toward read pages, beyond the comparison group's, is only suggestive. 83% of topic reading meets unchanged opening paragraphs.
*See the chart: "Reach".*

**3. Most added text is about older history, not the war.** Removing sentences about events since 2022 leaves 89% of the extra change. It replicated on held-back data; on the full set one of two intervals includes zero.
*See the chart: "News or narrative?"*

**4. The change was concentrated in 2024.** Critical terms went in at about 7 per 1,000 edits before October 2023, about 16 through 2024, and 5 to 6 in 2026. This describes data to late 2026, not a forecast.
*See the chart: "When it happened".*

**5. Established editors made most changes.** Accounts with under 500 edits made 9.1% of war-period topic edits (comparison: 17.2%). Later-blocked accounts made 13.7% of insertions against a 12.3% baseline, within the margin of error.
*See the chart: "How it spread" ("Established editors" panel).*

## Changes in both directions

The Nakba article, 2021: "The Nakba ... was the destruction of Palestinian society and homeland in 1948 ...". 2026: "The Nakba ... is the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists ...". The 2021 version also took a position; neither is "neutral".

In the other direction:
- Antisemitism appears in 54 topic opening paragraphs, up from 37.
- 61 critical terms left topic opening paragraphs (against 223 that entered); the 1948 expulsion article dropped "by Zionist militias".
- Terms attributed to a source ("according to") rose from 21% to 25% (comparison: 17%, flat). A few prominent opening paragraphs later dropped the attribution.

## Methods in one paragraph

We counted fixed lists of critical and Jewish-history terms in the opening paragraphs of 10,320 articles over their revision histories. Each result is the change in topic articles minus the change in comparison articles, which removes ordinary editing trends. Intervals resample articles and, separately, WikiProjects; where they disagree we call a result suggestive. Half the articles were held back until the end; the main result replicated there (+3.6 percentage points, interval 1.7 to 5.6). Measures failing precision checks, including one on 1948 departures, were withdrawn.

## Limitations and what this cannot tell us

- **Accuracy.** Word counts measure emphasis, not correctness.
- **Intent and coordination.** We see what changed, not why, and cannot see off-wiki coordination.
- **The average.** A pre-specified average test on a smaller panel found no clear rise; it was too weak to detect concentrated change.
- **Readers.** We know what text was live, not what readers took from it.
- **Languages.** English and Portuguese moved; nine other languages showed no clear change.

## What would change these conclusions

- A renewed rise in the quarterly insertion rate.
- A similar rise across a random sample of Wikipedia (not yet run), which would make this site-wide drift.
- Frequent false matches, such as "expulsion" in Jewish-history articles.
- A much lower reading share once the few largest pages are removed.

## Glossary

- **Opening paragraph:** the summary at the top of an article.
- **Comparison articles:** other countries, conflicts and religions, measured the same way.
- **Jewish-history vocabulary:** homeland, indigenous, antisemitism, Land of Israel; also counted.
- **Own voice vs attributed:** "it is genocide" versus "X states it is genocide".
