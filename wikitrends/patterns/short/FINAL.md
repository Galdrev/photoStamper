# The average didn't move.
## The window display did.

**The short answer.** Partly: most articles, Judaism's included, did not change, but a few hundred heavily read Israel and Palestine pages now open with a different baseline story, centred on 1948, the land and more critical vocabulary.

*Critical vocabulary:* words from the legal and historical charges made against Israel, such as genocide, apartheid, ethnic cleansing, occupation, colonial, Nakba/expulsion.

## The image

In a big shop the aisles are almost unchanged. But the window display by the front door is different now. The opening paragraph is that window: what readers, search engines and AI assistants see first.

What the encyclopedia states as its baseline for the Nakba. 2021: "The Nakba ... was the destruction of Palestinian society and homeland in 1948 ..." 2026: "The Nakba ... is the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists ..." The 2021 version also took a position; neither is "neutral".

## What changed the other way

- Antisemitism is mentioned in 54 topic opening paragraphs, up from 37.
- 61 critical terms left topic opening paragraphs (223 went in); the 1948 expulsion article dropped "by Zionist militias".
- More of these terms now name a source ("according to"): 21% to 25%. Few opening paragraphs use Wikipedia's own voice.

Lopsided, not one-sided.

## What matters

**1. Most articles did not change, and articles on Judaism did not move.**
85% of topic articles ended with the same count of critical vocabulary; Judaism moved no more than comparison articles.
*Chart: scrolly vocabulary chart.*

**2. The pages that changed are among the most read.**
Pages whose opening paragraph gained critical vocabulary get 15% of topic reading.
*Chart: "Reach".*

**3. The story moved back to 1948 and the land, not to the war.**
Without sentences about recent events, 89% of the extra change remains.
*Chart: "News or narrative?" (89/11 bar).*

**4. It came as one wave, and as of late 2026 the wave has passed.**
In 2024 critical vocabulary went in over twice as often as before the war; by 2026 it was back to the pre-war pace.
*Chart: "When it happened" (half-year bars).*

**5. Long-standing editors made the changes, not a wave of new accounts.**
Low-experience accounts made about half the share of edits they make on comparison pages.
*Chart: "How it spread" ("Established editors" panel).*

## Why you can trust this

- Every result is compared with about 5,700 articles on other countries, conflicts and religions, measured the same way, so ordinary Wikipedia churn cancels out.
- The average across all articles barely moved. The change concentrated in a minority of heavily read pages, which held up on the sealed half of the data: half the articles we set aside and only checked at the end.
- Filtering out sentences about recent events left most change: not just war news.
- We dropped our own failed results, such as one on 1948 departures.

## What this does not mean

- Not truth. Counting words measures emphasis, not accuracy.
- Not bias or intent. We see what changed, not why.
- Not coordination. Off-wiki planning is invisible to us.

We can't say who is right. We can say the front door changed.

## Questions people ask

**So is Wikipedia biased?** We cannot say. We show which story is now the baseline, not whether it is right.

**Who did it?** Long-standing accounts; later-blocked accounts made no more of these edits than usual. That rules out a newcomer flood, but says nothing about intent or off-wiki coordination.

**Is it just the war?** Battles brought editors; court rulings did not. Then editors mostly rewrote older history.

**Only in English?** English and Portuguese moved; nine other languages showed no clear change.

**Does anyone read it?** Yes, the changed pages are read ones, though 83% of topic reading meets unchanged opening paragraphs.

## Glossary

- **Opening paragraph:** the summary at the top of an article.
- **Comparison articles:** other countries and conflicts, measured the same way.
- **Jewish-history vocabulary:** homeland, indigenous, antisemitism, Land of Israel; counted too, so change either way shows.
- **Own voice vs attributed:** "it is genocide" versus "X states it is genocide".
