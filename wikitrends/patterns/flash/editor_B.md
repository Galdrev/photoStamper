## 0. The arc, in one breath

The research page currently runs to **18.6 phone screens** on the story tab, plus about 64 in the evidence room. Every section in it is true. What it lacks is a spine. The flash edition gets one spine in three movements:

1. **What the world reads now** (cards 1–2): the new first lines, put against the old ones.
2. **How it got there** (cards 3–7): one sentence fought over 292 times, and who won it; the battles each change followed; the same move in other languages; the overall score, the voting camps, and what Wikipedia's own arbitrators found about outside influence.
3. **Why it matters for Israel** (close): these are the lines millions read first, and the denial is now an afterthought or absent.

Every card has the same anatomy, so the reader learns it once. There is a headline (two sentences, then → now, exact words), then one proof object, then one focus line («then” → “now» with the changed words highlighted), then at most 50 words of body, then a link into the evidence room. The shock comes from putting old text next to new text. I use no adjectives that the quotes do not supply.

Target length: **about 8 phone screens** (estimates per card below). The hero stays almost as it is, because it already works.

## 7. Flags for the messaging agent and the editor-in-chief

- **F1. “The opposite is undone within a day” is a Zionism result, not a subject-wide one.** The research page itself says so (`#ev-zionism` More): across 254 core articles, removals of critical terms were *not* reverted faster (median 1.13 h vs 0.84 h). Also, 47 of the 78 hardening edits were *also* gone within a day. So the honest form is the paired comparison, “64 of the 70 … against 47 of the 78”, never “critical wording stays, the opposite is undone.” Card 3 follows this.
- **F2. One duration measure only (must-fix 9).** I use “gone within a day” (on the page) plus “37 of 41 removals of colonization undone” (on the page). If the editor-in-chief wants a days figure, CODES.md's headline cell gives *days live* 336 vs 38. Do not mix it with *stance-regime days* (650 vs 74), and do not write “about N times as long” without naming the measure.
- **F3. “Hostages” wording.** The October 7 opening *does* name the hostages (sentence 9: “251 Israeli and non-Israeli civilians and soldiers were taken as hostages”). Only its *first sentence* never does (107 versions), and only the *Israel and Hamas* openings never do (every snapshot since 2021, four copies). Every hostage line must keep that scope. The hero and dinner sentence say “Israel and Hamas articles.” Strictly, that should be “opening sections”: tighten it if space allows.
- **F4. Card 8's in/out bar is a new drawing from existing numbers** (223/61, 125/73, `#against .cap`). No recompute is needed, but the builder must draw it. “Almost four” = 3.66 and “fewer than two” = 1.71: both wordings are already on the page.
- **F5. “Several countries” Hamas designation (31 Oct 2026) may be reverted.** Keep the page's caveat on `#o7Pos`. Before publishing, re-check whether it still holds. The headline does not depend on it, since sentence 22 holds either way.
- **F6. “No top article” in the hero** means the 150 most-read opening sections that existed in 2021, at January 2021 and July 2023. The footnote or tooltip must say that. Do not stretch it to “no article.”
- **F7. Card 2 headline: Hamas “militant” → “political organisation with a military wing.”** “Militant” is still in the 2021 text («“fundamentalist but pragmatic, militant, and nationalist organization”»). Quote with the ellipsis as `#reads` does. Do not write “a militant group” as if it were a quote.
- **F8. ArbCom.** Quote only findings 10 and 16, verbatim, linked. No sides, and no link from Ïvana to our camps or network (must-fix 10/11). The voting camps' direction is not coded, and the card must say so.
- **F9. Spanish quote in Carousel C.** The `#xrFig` diff view interleaves the old and new Spanish text. Take the *current* wording from the stored text, not from the rendered diff.

## 8. Why this order (reasoning, kept for the round-2 review)

- **Lead with the verdict, then the receipts.** The reader gets the whole story in the hero and Carousel A in about two screens. Everything after explains *how*. A reader who stops after card 2 still leaves with the dinner sentence.
- **Zionism comes before 1948 and October 7** because it is the only place where we can show the *mechanism*, a tug of war with a winner, in exact words. Once the reader has seen one sentence fought over 292 times, every later then/now reads as the outcome of a fight, not a spontaneous drift.
- **The battles come after the three subject cards, not first.** The research page ran timing as its own thesis. Here it is the hinge between “what changed” and “who did it.” It turns the changes into a dated chronicle without claiming cause.
- **The languages come before the camps** so that “who” is the last question asked, and the answer is Wikipedia's own arbitration committee, in its own words. That is the strongest true statement of outside influence we have, and it closes the investigation on a primary source, not on our statistics.
- **The opposite-direction edits sit inside the scoreboard**, never as a “balance” card, per must-fix 1/2. They are shown, with their proportion.
- **The close returns to the hero's quote structure** (sentence, then denial) and to reach, which is the honest answer to “why it matters”: these are the first lines millions read.
