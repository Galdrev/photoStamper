# Flash edition v1, round-2 review: Editor B (story)

I read scratchpad/flash/index.html start to finish as a reader would. I used an iPhone 13 at 390×844 (it measures 8.08 screens) and also checked 1280. I read it against messaging_check.md (its vetoes are binding and I keep every one) and BUILD_v1.md. No new numbers: every figure below is already on the flash page or in the research edition. "Exact new wording" is given in «».

## Verdict as a reader

- **The "what" lands.** In the first two screens a reader gets the core shock: genocide in Israel's own article, Zionism "through colonization," and the Nakba "by Zionists." Cards 1–5 are strong, exact and well anchored. Card 5 (the charge both ways) is the best juxtaposition on the page.
- **The "how" is half there.** Card 6 shows what stays in four first sentences. But the battles, which link events to wording, survive only as slide 5 of 5 and as one clause in card 4.
- **The "why it matters" is buried.** The reach line is the fourth paragraph of the close, below a ratio line, after a 7-slide carousel that repeats cards 1–5.
- **The cards do not connect.** Each one starts cold, so the piece reads as nine separate exhibits, not an investigation.
- **The opposite-direction edits are hidden.** They sit on slide 7 of 7, which very few readers reach. This is an honesty problem as well as a story problem: must-fix 1/2 wants them shown with their proportion, and the proportion line sits under the carousel, apart from them.
- **The "In one sentence" box holds four sentences.**

The fixes below cost about zero net screens. Cutting the close carousel (change 2) pays for the transitions (change 3).

## The 12 changes, ranked

### 1. Close: put "why it matters" first and name it (`#close`)
Reorder the close to: headline → why it matters → the score plus the edits that went the other way (change 2) → honesty line → dinner sentence → buttons. Replace the reach paragraph with:
> «**Why it matters.** These lines sit at the top of the most-read pages that changed: Benjamin Netanyahu, 7.0 million views in the past 12 months; Israel, 4.6 million; Palestine, 2.4 million; Zionism, 1.8 million; Hamas, 1.3 million.»

Why: this is the third movement of the arc, and right now it reads as an afterthought. "At the top of the page" describes where the opening section literally sits, so it does not bring back the vetoed "the world reads first" superlative. The figures and the "most-read pages that changed" label are unchanged (messaging F11).

### 2. Close: cut carousel 3 and show the edits that went Israel's way, without a swipe (`#car3`)
Carousel 3 repeats the hero and cards 1–5 slide for slide (Israel, Zionism, Nakba, October 7, Hamas, Gaza genocide), and its only new material is hidden on slide 7. Delete the carousel and replace it with a link, «Every line, then and now →» to `#reads`. Directly under the score line, put a visible block:
> «**The score.** For every critical term taken out of these opening paragraphs, almost four went in: 223 in, 61 out. On comparison pages (other countries, conflicts and religions), 125 in, 73 out.
> **Some lines moved the other way.** The 1948 Palestinian expulsion and flight: “by Zionist militias” left its first sentence on 30 May 2025; the next sentence names “the Zionist paramilitaries Haganah, Irgun, and Lehi.” October 7 attacks: on 1 October 2026 its second sentence called Israel's response “actions scholars have concluded constitute genocide”; by 2 November the clause was gone. The removal is recent and may be undone.»

Why: this saves about 0.25 screen. It puts the opposite edits next to their proportion, which is exactly the must-fix 1/2 form. It also removes the largest redundancy on the page.

### 3. Add a one-line transition at the foot of each card (cards 1–7, above "Full evidence →")
Use small italic, one line on a phone. Exact lines:
- end of card 1: «The same kind of change reached 1948.»
- end of card 2: «And it reached October 7.»
- end of card 3: «The group that carried out the attack got a new definition too.»
- end of card 4: «Then came the charge itself.»
- end of card 5: «How did these lines get there? In four first sentences, the history shows which wording stayed and which was taken out.»
- end of card 6: «Who edits this topic? Wikipedia's own arbitrators looked.»
- end of card 7: «What a reader opening these pages finds today:»

Why: this is what turns nine exhibits into one story. None of the lines makes a claim or uses a connective that implies cause (card 6 → 7 asks a question and does not answer it with "so" or "because", as messaging §2 requires).

### 4. "In one sentence" must be one sentence (`#close`, dinner box)
> «Before the war, none of the most-read Wikipedia openings on the subject charged Israel with genocide; now Israel's own article says, in Wikipedia's voice, that Israel ‘began committing genocide,’ Zionism is ‘a Jewish homeland through colonization,’ and the Israel and Hamas opening sections never mention the hostages.»

The words are the same as the build's; only the punctuation joins them. The build's quote assertion still passes.

### 5. Hero: remove the duplicate quote (`header.hero`, the WHAT CHANGED box and the deck)
The first screen shows the same sentence twice: once in the hero quote card and again in the WHAT CHANGED box. "Since 4 May 2025" also appears twice. Shrink the box to the absence line only:
> WHAT CHANGED: «Jan 2021, Jul 2023: no genocide charge in the 150 most-read opening sections → since 4 May 2025: the sentence above.»

Deck:
> «Stated in Wikipedia's own voice. The article was viewed 4.6 million times in the past 12 months. Below: the other core articles, then and now.»

This keeps the then → now focus line (must-fix 12) without repeating the quote.

### 6. Card 6: say which four sentences, and mark it as the start of "how" (`#c6` eyebrow and body)
A reader hits "These are four sentences" without having been told which four. Eyebrow:
> «6 · HOW IT GOT THERE: FOUR FIRST SENTENCES»

First sentence of the body:
> «These are four first sentences, Gaza genocide, Hamas, October 7 and Zionism, not the whole subject.»

The rest stays verbatim. It carries the "no faster across the 254" line, which is binding.

### 7. Card 6: put the battles slide first in carousel 2 (`#car2`)
Move slide 5 ("When · new critical terms a week", `#kChart`) to position 1, and re-caption it:
> «In the three weeks after October 7, 13.7 new critical terms a week, against 3.8 in the six weeks before. Timing gives a date, not a cause. The next slides show four first sentences, word by word.»

Then come the Gaza genocide, Hamas, October 7 and Zionism lanes.

Why: the owner loves this chart, and must-fix 4 calls the link from events to wording "the interesting part". At slide 5 almost nobody sees it. At slide 1 it opens the "how" movement. **Condition:** the card 6 headline is about the Gaza genocide lane. If the editor-in-chief keeps that headline, put the Gaza genocide lane at slide 1 and the chart at slide 2. Do not bury it at 5.

### 8. Card 1: replace the redundant body with "who won" (`#c1` body)
The current body repeats a chip ("as few Palestinian Arabs as possible") and the phase-3 line, and both appear again on carousel 2, slide 4. New body:
> «Of the 41 removals of “colonization” we can check, 37 were undone. It has stood since 27 August 2026.»

Why: this is the shortest true statement of who won the Zionism sentence. It is one measure, "undone", which follows messaging F2/F10. The phase row above already carries 33 in / 32 out and "taken out and put back 10 times".

### 9. Card 5, slide 5: stop repeating card 4's Hebrew Hamas line (`#car1`, slide 5)
Card 4 already shows Hebrew calling Hamas a "terror organisation". Cut the second paragraph of slide 5 down to October 7 only:
> «Its October 7 article opens: “The Seventh of October massacre was a large-scale surprise attack…” In 107 versions of English's first sentence, “massacre” lasted about an hour in all.»

Keep the South Africa case part of the slide as it is. Both quotes and the 107/hour figure are already on the page.

### 10. Card 7: say "no side" once (`#c7`)
"They named no side" (headline) and "Neither finding names a side" (body) say the same thing. Delete the body sentence «Neither finding names a side.» Keep «That is camp voting, not proof of coordination, and we did not code which side each group took.» The headline already carries the no-side statement, where messaging §2 wants it.

### 11. Close headline: match its scope to the opening sections (`#close` H2)
"These first lines" is loose, because the Israel quote is from the opening section, not the first sentence. New headline:
> «These openings once said “re-establishment” (2021), “militant” (2021) and “coordinated attacks” (October 2023). Now they say “colonization,” “ethnic cleansing … by Zionists” and “genocide.”»

The eyebrow «WHAT THE WORLD READS NOW» can stay. It now closes the loop with the hero instead of opening a recap.

### 12. Card 3: make the "Hamas is named" clause read as fairness, not filler (`#c3` body)
As written, "and Hamas is named later in the same first sentence" sounds like a stray fact. Reorder the body so the fairness point comes first and the sharp point lands last:
> «The opening still says “massacring civilians in 21 communities,” and the first sentence names Hamas, later on. But the rape sentence that in January 2024 named “Hamas fighters” names no one on 2 November 2026.»

Same quotes and dates as the build. "But" contrasts two of the article's own sentences, so it implies no cause.

## What I would not change
- **The order of cards 1–5**, and card 5 as the emotional peak.
- **Card 4's Hebrew/English mirror.** It is the best image for an Israeli reader.
- **The card 6 scope line about the 254 articles.** It is binding, and it also makes the card more credible.
- **Card 7's two-fact structure, with no direction colour.**
- **The honesty line, as built.**

## Reader test, after these changes
- **Does the arc hold?** Yes. What: hero to card 5. How: card 6 with the battles first, then card 7. Why it matters: the opening of the close. The transitions make the joins visible.
- **Can the reader repeat the dinner sentence?** Yes. Its three parts appear in the hero (genocide), card 1 (colonization) and card 3 (hostages), in the same order as the sentence, and the close states it once as one sentence.
- **Screens.** Cutting carousel 3 (about −0.25) roughly offsets the seven transition lines (about +0.2), and changes 5, 8 and 10 save a few more lines, so the total should stay at about 8.
