# Skeptic's stress test: the Israeli-media angle, attacked from both sides

Reader: the same sharp, skeptical professional as before, now playing two hostile critics.
- **Critic L ("hit piece")**: "An Israeli outlet cherry-picks the loudest sentences, blames anonymous volunteers and a Discord server, and ignores that the world's courts and scholars say these things."
- **Critic R ("whitewash")**: "A study that says 'not detected', 'not proof of bias' and 'Israel's share barely moved' is laundering a pro-Palestinian takeover with statistics."

Read: ANGLE.md, PERP.md, OCT7.md (first half only: cards 1 to 4 plus the method paragraph), ORGS.md, WHO.md (Part 1 and the start of Part 2), AUDIT.md, the text of page_template.html (about 30k characters of prose; I did not read the 316 KB of scripts and data). I did not read the second half of OCT7.md, the tail of WHO.md, or footprints.json. I read the first 5,000 characters of orgs/tests.md only. Sourcing caveat from ORGS.md applies: every organisation item was read as a search snippet, not full text.

## (1) The 10 most attackable claims or framings

Each: attack, who makes it, fix.

**1. "Wikipedia's own voice now says Israel 'began committing genocide'." (ANGLE.md, PERP Card 2; the lead of the whole angle.)**
- Attack (R): you quote one sentence and omit the next one, "Israel has denied that its actions constitute genocide." You also omit that the other 5 genocide sentences are attributed to scholars and bodies, and that the claim stands on an ICJ case and a "growing scholarly consensus".
- Attack (L): 2 of 8 sentences, snapshot taken on 5 Nov 2026, from a lead that changes weekly. "Now" is a day.
- Fix: reword to "In two leads, Israel and Benjamin Netanyahu, the encyclopedia states the genocide charge in its own voice. The Israel lead follows it with Israel's denial. In five others it attributes the charge." Add the snapshot date and "as of 5 Nov 2026; leads change daily". Keep the quote exact. Do not write "declares" or "accuses Israel of". Cut "Netanyahu" from the headline unless the BLP edit history is checked. Say how long each sentence has stood (the data has revision history for it).

**2. "A few hundred pages ... 15% of reading ... three times the comparison." (page, "Few pages, many readers".)**
- Attack (R and L both): this does not survive the audit. Against other live conflicts the exposure gap disappears: 15.0% vs 10.5% (conflict projects, CI of the gap −4.8 to +12.7) and 14.1% (Ukraine/Russia/Armenia/Azerbaijan, gap +0.9, CI −10.3 to +13.6). The "5%" comparison is a pooled group including religions and quiet countries. A reader who checks will say the study picked the flattering yardstick.
- Fix: cut "three times". Say "about 14% of topic reading, similar to what other active conflicts show". Move the exposure finding from the lead to a subordinate position. The finding becomes "the pages that changed are the ones people read, as on other conflicts", which is true and less tabloid.

**3. "We collected every English Wikipedia article in the topic, 4,642 of them." (page.)**
- Attack: false by the audit's own account. It is 4,642 sampled Top, High and Mid pages of 4 WikiProjects (about 9,200 in the frame). Low-importance and unrated pages (villages, minor biographies) are not covered. 572 overlapping pages were dropped, probably the most entangled (Iran-Israel, Lebanon). Zionism, Arab-Israeli conflict and Jewish culture contributed nothing to the frame. "Followed every edit to 10,320 articles" also overstates.
- Fix: use the audit's wording: "Articles that Wikipedia's own WikiProjects Israel, Palestine, Judaism and Jewish history rate Top, High or Mid, about 9,200; we analysed 4,642." Remove "every". Fix 5,678 vs 5,670 (audit says 5,670, plus 8 atrocity articles).

**4. "Judaism did not move" used as proof of fairness; "Israel and Palestine ... 61% of articles, 84% of additions".**
- Attack (L): of course Judaism did not move; that is a control chosen to make the rest look targeted, and "Israel and Palestine pages" were already contested. Attack (R): the 61% and 84% figures are sample shares. Weighted to the population, Israel+Palestine are 67% of articles and the gap falls from +5.2 to +2.7 pp.
- Fix: say "the change is in Israel and Palestine articles, not in Judaism or Jewish-history articles" and keep the weighted number next to the unweighted. State that Judaism is a negative control, not a verdict.

**5. "89% of the extra change is about 1948 and the land, not the war." (page, all drafts.)**
- Attack: the audit shows the ratio is unstable once weighted: 123% [−470, 787]. Removing one Karabakh village (Martuni) gives 81% [57, 94]. Critic R: "so the headline is a coin-flip." Critic L: "so what the readers see is what the war made editors rewrite; you cannot separate the war from the retelling."
- Fix: "most of the change (roughly 80 to 90%, depending on how the comparison pages are weighted) is in sentences that are not about recent events." Add one line: the war brought the editors, and they rewrote older history.

**6. "Occupation moved from attributed to stated as fact (46% to 80% own voice)." (PERP Card 3.)**
- Attack (L): the ICJ advisory opinion (July 2024) is a real source, and the Wikipedia change follows it. Attack (R): Card 3 itself shows Russia/Crimea went 25% to 74%.
- Fix: print the Russia/Crimea comparison next to it, mention the ICJ opinion in the same sentence, and do not present this as Israel being singled out. The audit's rule (an Israeli critic should not present this as singling out Israel) should be enforced in the copy.

**7. "Hamas lost 'militant' / 'terrorist' dropped / designation moved to the end of the lead." (ANGLE.md, OCT7 Cards 1 to 3.)**
- Attack (L): "militant" became "military", which is arguably more accurate; "terrorist" never held in the definition (OCT7 itself corrects the brief). Attack (R): ISIS, al-Shabaab and Boko Haram lost "terrorist" from the first sentence too; this is Wikipedia style, not an Israel rule. Also the lead grew from 154 to 525 words, so any sentence moves later. The "Several countries" edit is 5 days old and may be reverted.
- Fix: do not write "terrorist moved out of Hamas's definition". Use OCT7's own precise version: "Hamas, Hezbollah and PIJ are now introduced as political organisations with armed wings, while ISIS and al-Qaeda are still introduced as militant organisations." Say the designation sentence is now second-to-last. Drop the "Several countries" edit from any headline, or label it "five days old, may be reverted". Give the date and hour for "militant" (12 Oct 2023) only if the lead-time view data is stated.

**8. "Documented off-wiki canvassing (the Discord channel, confirmed by Wikipedia's own arbitration body)." (ANGLE.md.)**
- Attack (R): ArbCom sanctioned individuals; it did not adopt "40 editors" or "hijacked". ORGS.md itself lists "8,000 articles" as a probable conflation with Discord members, and says the mailing-list part is only an allegation in a request. Attack (L): you lead with the pro-Palestinian channel; ORGS.md documents pro-Israel canvassing and proxy editing for a banned user (Jan 2024), CAMERA (2008), the Yesha/My Israel course, Heritage's "Project Esther" plan.
- Fix: "ArbCom has made off-wiki coordination findings on both sides" is the sentence. Lead with that. Name the Discord channel with ArbCom's exact findings (private-case sanctions, December 2024; further site bans, April 2025), not press figures. Say that ArbCom did not publish the size of either group. Verify the open items in ORGS.md Section 5 before printing (PIA5 remedies, motion text, dates).

**9. "Established editors, not newcomers" / "experienced regulars wrote it". (WHO Card 7, PERP, page.)**
- Attack: the topic is under the extended-confirmed restriction (30 days, 500 edits); non-EC accounts can only post edit requests. So "9.1% of edits from accounts under 500 edits" is partly a rule, not a finding, and the comparison pages (17.2%) are mostly not under the rule. Also WHO Card 7 says most of those regulars had not edited that page before the war (7 to 13%), which readers will hear as "recruited". WHO Card 6 shows blocked accounts removed critical terms before the war more than expected (27.2% vs 20.2%); a critic can read either side into that.
- Fix: add "this topic only allows accounts with 500+ edits, so newcomers are barred by rule" next to the finding. Say plainly "the war drew experienced Wikipedians new to these pages", not "regulars". Do not state or imply sockpuppetry; drop "later-blocked" lines from the main text.

**10. "The Zionism definition now says 'through colonization'; 292 edits, 108 later undone; the sentence did not settle until August 2026." (page, ANGLE.md.)**
- Attack: the page counts 108 undone; WHO Card 2 says "colonization" went in 44 times and out 41, and 63 of 85 flips in 2024 (12 in 2025, 10 in 2026). So it is still flipping, and "settled on 27 August 2026" is 10 weeks of stability. Also the Zionism quote pair is the most selective one in the project: 2021's sentence ("re-establishment of ... a Jewish state") is itself a sympathetic self-description, so the comparison is "pro-Zionist wording to critical wording", not "neutral to biased". Also the "Jewish 're-establishment' link was removed" claim in ANGLE.md I could not check against any file I read.
- Fix: say "settled since 27 Aug 2026 (as of 5 Nov)". Print the 2021 sentence in full, and say what each version is sourced to (check the edit summaries). Show 2021 lead of at least one other contested ideology (a comparison "movement" article) so readers see the baseline. Remove the "link removed" line unless the revision diff is shown.

**Honourable mentions.**
- Hamas ("a political organisation with a military wing", 12 Oct 2023) is not "five days after the massacre" in any causal sense. The data show timing only.
- PERP Card 1: Israel's share as perpetrator moved 23.6 to 26.4%, not statistically clear. Any copy saying Wikipedia "blames Israel more" is overclaiming. The finding is the charge, not the share.
- PERP coding was done and checked by the same coder (Claude), 98 to 99% on 100 rows. A hostile reader will say "an AI graded its own homework". Disclose it in one line.

## (2) What the coordination network graph must show to be credible

The page currently has no graph; it has a sentence ("a report alleging coordinated editing. The Wikimedia Foundation disputed its conclusions"). A graph will become the most-screenshotted object, so it needs to be bullet-proof against both critics. Requirements:

1. **Nodes are anonymous and aggregate.** Hashed accounts, no names, no per-person profiles (ANGLE.md privacy rule). Link hashes only within data where that is valid: WHO.md says the hashes link edits only within one source file, and in mass2 only within one article. A cross-article network is therefore only possible for the 25 key articles (216 traced changes, 151 accounts). Say so on the graph.
2. **Null model, shown on the same canvas.** A drawn network always looks like a conspiracy. Show the observed overlap beside a popularity-preserving random null (same number of editors per article, same edit counts). WHO.md already has this: top-150 overlap rose 1.11x to 1.61x of null on topic pages (comparison 1.17x to 1.08x). Show the whole null distribution (many random draws) and the observed value, not just a ratio.
3. **Comparison network, same method, same size.** Same extraction on the comparison conflicts (Ukraine, Armenia/Azerbaijan, Kurdistan, etc.), with the same number of articles, editors and time window. Note that "0 accounts on 3+ comparison key articles" vs "9 on topic" is not like for like: key articles on each side were chosen differently (topic: press-documented and hand-picked; comparison: hand-picked). Re-run on random same-importance articles or label it as a case-study contrast.
4. **Both-directions test.** Colour edges or groups by direction of change (inserting critical terms vs removing) and show both camps. WHO Card 5 already says insert and remove are done by largely separate accounts (318 only inserted, 296 only removed, 59 both), and the comparison is the same split (10.7% vs 8.8% did both). Show that the "removers" camp is as network-clustered as the "inserters". If only one side is drawn, critic R wins by default. Where you can't tell direction, say so.
5. **Sample sizes on the figure itself.** Number of articles, accounts, edits, events (5,053 critical-term events), time windows, and the fact that traced changes are one per quarter (n=16 for Zionism), not every edit. Every cell with fewer than 10 events should be greyed.
6. **The detection limit, in the caption.** Say what the test could have seen. org_tests: a one-sided 5% test needs the topic/comparison ratio to fall to 0.20x or rise to 4.2x. Zero pairs edited within 30 minutes on 5+ articles in both groups. This design cannot see off-wiki coordination, talk-page and RfC votes (where canvassing usually happens), or sockpuppets never caught. "Not detected" must not appear without "this test could not detect it".
7. **Timeline element.** Mark the dated organisational events (CAMERA 2008, Jan 2024 pro-Israel canvassing motions, TfP channel Jan 2024, Pirate Wires 24 Oct 2024, PIA5 23 Jan 2025, April 2025 bans), and show the 3.4x surge before the channel existed (7 Oct 2023 to 1 Feb 2024) vs 2.0x while active and 1.8x after. That is the firmest result in the files and it cuts against "Discord caused it". It does not show the Discord added nothing.
8. **Documented vs alleged encoded visually.** Solid edge = documented (ArbCom finding, or the organisation's own announcement); dashed = alleged (press, ADL, Pirate Wires). Dotted = disputed or likely error (the 8,000 articles). The 3 results that "point the right way" in org_tests (Jan 2024 motions, PIA5, April 2025) are at best suggestive and none survives correction for 13 tests. Do not draw them as findings.
9. **Both camps with equal visual weight.** Pro-Israel items (CAMERA, Yesha/My Israel, Israel Group, Heritage plan, Jan 2024 canvassing) must be on the graph, not a footnote. ANGLE.md allows them to not lead, but the graph is not the lead; it is the evidence.
10. **No individual unmasking.** Even hashed, the per-article top-account share (e.g. 38.8% on Nakba) can identify a person to the community. Aggregate or round.

## (3) Where the Israeli-media framing risks overclaiming

- **"Name these plainly ... 'the encyclopedia now states as fact'." (ANGLE.md.)** "The encyclopedia" is thousands of volunteers; the sentence is "English Wikipedia's opening paragraph for X reads ... as of date". "States as fact" is wrong for the attributed cases and arguable for the others (the denial follows).
- **"How the world sees Israel."** Wikipedia is not the world. English only; Portuguese moved too; 9 other languages showed no clear change. Say "what English-language readers and AI tools are shown first".
- **Causation from reading.** "Search engines and AI assistants quote the opening" is an assertion with no reader-behaviour data. We cannot say what readers came to believe (draft C said this right).
- **"Pro-Palestinian takeover" implication.** The data contain no author identity or viewpoint. WHO Card 5: the camps are separate, same as elsewhere. ArbCom banned about 8 (6 pro-Palestinian, 2 pro-Israel as reported) in PIA5 on conduct grounds. Any copy that turns "Discord channel exists" into "Wikipedia was captured" overclaims.
- **"Terrorist" claims.** ANGLE.md's own bullet was corrected in OCT7: "terrorist" was never in the definition. Printing the brief's version would be a factual error a critic will find in ten minutes.
- **The 2026 ADL and Pirate Wires numbers** (40 editors, 95% decline, 8 original + 11 new) are allegations from outlets with a position; WMF disputed them. Never use them as our figures.
- **One-directional selection of examples.** The best pairs table is nearly all Israel-critical. Print the counter-examples (the Gaza-Israel lead stating Palestinian militants committed war crimes in own voice; Goldstein as "a Jewish terrorist and mass murderer"; "massacres and acts of terror conducted by both sides"; Hamas's lead saying it attacks civilians; the antisemitism count 37 to 54 in the earlier draft). These are PERP's own, and they are the single best defence against critic L.
- **"This is the headline" language** in the PERP so-what, plus phrases like "house style" and "the two lines did not move by the same rules", are editorial. Keep them out of the published copy or label them as our reading.
- **Hand-picked case studies read as typical.** The 25 to 40 key articles and the deep panel were chosen because they are contested (the audit: selection on the outcome). Always label them "case studies", never "the trend".
- **Israeli-government framing.** The brief says "What Israelis should understand about how the world sees them". A critic will read that as national grievance. Frame as "what the reference work tells the world about the conflict, and how it changed".

## (4) Verdict on the sampling correction

**I accept the audit.** It is the most credible document in the folder: it finds its own comparison group flawed, reports a result that cuts against the project's exposure claim, and gives reproducible scripts. Its core conclusions:
- Unweighted figures describe the sample. Weighting to the 4 projects' population roughly halves the article-share results and leaves exposure and the "most of the change is older history" finding intact (at about 80 to 90%).
- Matching on prominence goes the other way and makes the gap bigger (+2.8).
- Best single population estimate: (a) about +1.8 per 10k words and (b) about +1.9 pp, smaller than published, clearly non-zero.

**Criticism of the correction itself (what a hostile reader will say).**
- The weighted estimates are post hoc and exploratory (the audit says so; a SPEC amendment is not yet written). They must be labelled that way in print or pre-registered before publication.
- Cluster-bootstrap intervals (by project) cross zero for (a) {−0.44, 4.79} and weighted {−0.97, 4.32}, and for (d) {−0.6, 22.9}. With 4 topic projects and 23 comparison projects, a critic can argue we cannot distinguish the effect from "projects differ". We should say that once.
- Weighted comparison depends on one village (Martuni at weight 37). The audit's variants handle it, but report the range, not a point.
- Against conflict projects only, weighted (a) falls to +0.14 [−2.10, 1.61] and (b) to +0.3 pp. That is the strongest critic-R argument ("it's just what war does to Wikipedia"). We must say that this comparison is fragile because its Mid tier is a handful of Karabakh and Kurdish pages, and that matched on prominence the conflict-only effect is +2.77. Do not hide either number.
- The sample leaves out Low-importance pages entirely, where much of the topic may sit.

## Bottom line

The strongest honest story is smaller and sharper than the draft angle: a handful of the most-read Israel and Palestine leads now state grave charges in Wikipedia's own voice (genocide in two), 1948 is retold with an agent, and Hamas and its neighbours are introduced differently from jihadist groups; the same drift on "occupation" happened to Russia; the gap in reader exposure is not special against other conflicts; organised off-wiki editing is documented on both sides, and our data neither confirms nor rules out its effect. That version survives both critics. The current ANGLE.md version does not.
