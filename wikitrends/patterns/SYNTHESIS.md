# Synthesis: how English Wikipedia's coverage of Israel, Palestine, Zionism and Judaism changed, 2021 to 2026

Editor's synthesis of 38 pattern cards from three analysts (when_where_what = WWW, how_who = HW, story_sources_langs = SSL), checked against notes/FINDINGS.md, study3/holdout_results/SUMMARY.md and TALKING_POINTS.md. Card references look like WWW3 or HW8. Chart files are given relative to `wikitrends/patterns/`.

---

## The picture in 5 sentences

1. Between 2021 and 2026, English Wikipedia's Israel and Palestine articles took on more of the vocabulary of legal accusation and of 1948 (genocide, ethnic cleansing, expulsion, occupation), while Judaism articles did not move and comparison articles on other countries and conflicts moved much less.
2. The change came as one wave in the first half of 2024, months after the October 2023 news peak, and the article mass has been flat since early 2025; only a handful of flagship leads were still settling in 2026.
3. It is concentrated: 85% of topic articles did not change at all, but the change landed hardest in the leads and first sentences of the most-read pages, so an average over articles barely moves while what readers see did.
4. It came mostly as added sentences about older history and the land, written by experienced regulars on pages with a lot of talk-page discussion and few reverts, citing much the same sources as before and usually with attribution; a few high-traffic leads (Gaza genocide, Israel, Israeli apartheid, Nakba, Zionism) now state contested labels in Wikipedia's own voice.
5. Jewish history content was kept, the source mix barely shifted, other language editions mostly did not follow, and we found no sign of synchronized tag-teaming; the data cannot tell us whether the new wording is accurate, what editors intended, or whether anyone coordinated off-wiki.

## Headline patterns

### P1. The change came as one wave in the first half of 2024, and the article mass has been flat since 2025
*Merges WWW1, WWW5, WWW8, HW2, HW12, HW6. Holdout A9 (sustained addition rate) and A10 (post-war surge predicts gain) replicate.*

- **Numbers.** Net critical terms added per 100 topic articles per quarter: about 7 before the war, then **40 (2024-Q1) and 46 (2024-Q2)**, and about 4 in 2025-26. Pooled rate 13.3 to 16.0 per 10k words by Jan 2025, then **16.1 in Oct 2026**. Quiet comparison articles: 12.8 to 12.9. Pageviews of key articles were 22x normal in Oct 2023 and back to 2 to 3x within three months; edits stayed at 1.7 to 2.5x for 15 months.
- **Confidence.** STRONG for the wave and the plateau. SUGGESTIVE for "core first, periphery a year later" (WWW8; the Judaism periphery has only 38 added terms).
- **Best chart.** Bars of net terms added per 100 articles per quarter, topic against quiet comparison, with the pooled rate as a line; mark 7 Oct 2023 and the ICJ order of 26 Jan 2024. `when_where_what/chart_data/01_timeline_quarterly.json`. Second panel if time allows: `how_who/chart_data/views_vs_edits_index.json` (readers spike and leave, edits stay).
- **Example.** List of genocides went from 55 to 154 critical hits between the Apr and Jul 2024 snapshots, when the Gaza entry was written. Zionism's editing: 10.9 edits per 100k views before the war, 4.0 in the first six months, 42.8 after.
- **So what.** This was a time-limited reframing, not a continuing slide. "Wikipedia keeps getting more critical" is not supported; the step happened in 2024. Within each article, words and edits arrive in the same quarter (see D2). The size of the news spike does not predict the direction of change (Spearman 0.01 across 22 articles). Attention brought editors; it did not set the direction.

### P2. A few hundred pages carry the change; 85% of topic articles did not change, and Judaism did not move
*Merges WWW2, HW7 (in part), holdout A1, A5, A5b, B1 by project.*

- **Numbers.** Of 4,009 unique mass topic articles, **85.1% had exactly the same count**, and 456 gained (215 in the equal-length pre-war window). The top **40 articles (1%) supplied 38%** of added terms. Israel and Palestine projects are 61% of articles and supplied 84% of additions. Judaism is 28% of articles and supplied 7%. Replicated DiD: **+3.6 pp** more topic articles gained than comparison; Israel+Palestine minus Judaism +5.6 pp; Judaism vs comparison −0.1 pp [−2.3, 2.3], formally equivalent to zero.
- **Confidence.** STRONG (10k articles, comparison group, sealed-half replication).
- **Best chart.** Lorenz curve, four lines (topic pre/post, comparison pre/post). `when_where_what/chart_data/03_concentration.json`. Label the 1% and 5% points, not the 10% point (see data notes).
- **Example.** Biggest gainers, Jul 2023 to Nov 2026: Gaza Strip +25, Benjamin Netanyahu +22, Criticism of Israel +21, West Bank +18, Deir Yassin massacre +16.
- **So what.** "Wikipedia shifted on Israel" is really "a few hundred Israel and Palestine pages shifted". Articles on Jews, Judaism and religion are where they were. The spread did widen: gainers doubled and the top-1% share fell from 50% to 38%, while comparison gainers barely moved (302 to 341). Monitoring can be small and targeted.

### P3. The change sits where readers look: leads, first sentences, and the most-read pages
*Merges WWW6, SSL1, SSL7, SSL3, Q9 in SSL. Holdout A4 (opening-paragraph gains) and A6 (Top/High importance) replicate.*

- **Numbers.** Mass tier: critical rate in leads **+35%** (8.18 to 11.03 per 10k words) against **+13.5%** in bodies; comparison leads +5.4%. First sentences with a critical term: 1.48% to **2.08%** (comparison 0.63% to 0.66%). Deep panel: the **60 of 407** topic articles whose lead gained critical terms carry **46% of topic pageviews**; in comparison articles, the 24% that gained carry 18%.
- **Confidence.** STRONG for lead amplification and first sentences. STRONG for the views share as arithmetic, but a handful of very large pages drive it, and pageviews are by current title. SUGGESTIVE for SSL3 ("87% of views came before the wording settled"): it depends on defining "settled" as the last flip, and the new wording was often live part of the time before that.
- **Best chart.** Paired bars, share of articles vs share of pageviews, topic and comparison. `story_sources_langs/chart_data/exposure_concentration.json`. Back-up: `when_where_what/chart_data/09_lead_vs_body.json`.
- **Example.** Benjamin Netanyahu's lead went from 0 to 74 critical terms per 10k words (27M views). 2026: "Netanyahu's government has been orchestrating the Gaza genocide, culminating in the South Africa v. Israel case before the International Court of Justice (ICJ) in December 2023."
- **So what.** This is the twist. Count articles and the change is small, and the pre-registered average test was null. Count readers and it is large. Search snippets and AI answers draw on exactly these lead sentences. Even so, 98% of topic first sentences still carry none of these terms.

### P4. The story moved back to 1948 and to the land: places, villages, Zionism, Nakba and naming, more than war pages
*Merges WWW4, SSL6, SSL10, SSL11 (as anecdote), event filter, holdout B2, B3.*

- **Numbers.** Lead change in older-history sentences: **+8.4 of +9.3** per 10k words (event filter). History-anchored vs event-anchored added sentences: 18 vs 5 (holdout B2, replicates). Place articles gaining per year: **1.6% to 4.6%**; people 1.3% to 2.4%; religion under 1% throughout. **35 of 60** gaining place articles gained Nakba/expulsion wording. 'Southern Levant' in topic leads: 3 to 10 (comparison 0 to 0). First sentences naming 'Palestine': 30 to 43, while Jewish land names also rose (11 to 19, B3).
- **Confidence.** STRONG for the history-anchored residual (replicated). SUGGESTIVE for the article-type breakdown (classifier about 70% accurate) and for naming (small deep counts).
- **Best chart.** Paired horizontal bars, % gaining per year by article type, pre vs post, topic and comparison panels. `when_where_what/chart_data/05_geography_type_importance.json`. For the talk, pair it with a then/now card (`then_vs_now` in `story_sources_langs/cards.json`).
- **Example.** Nakba, 2021: "The Nakba, also known as the Palestinian Catastrophe, was the destruction of Palestinian society and homeland in 1948, and the permanent displacement of a majority of the Palestinian Arabs." 2026: "The Nakba ... is the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists through their violent displacement and dispossession of land, property, and belongings ..."
- **So what.** The war did not mainly produce war coverage. It sent editors back to retell 1897 to 1948 and to rename the land. That is a change in the historical record, and it reaches geography and biography pages that nobody thinks of as "conflict" pages. Jewish history content was kept (B1: ρ = −0.03 between critical gain and continuity). The connecting phrases in a few flagship first sentences ('birthplace', 'homeland') were trimmed, but the coded sample shows no net cut in Jewish belonging claims (D8).

### P5. The new words are genocide and expulsion; apartheid is an older layer; colonial is rising everywhere; terrorism thinned out
*Merges WWW3, WWW7, WWW11, SSL12.*

- **Numbers.** After July 2023, topic rates (deep / mass): **genocide +46% / +23%** (comparison −1% / +1%), ethnic cleansing +37% / +29%, Nakba/expulsion +22% / +17%. Apartheid rose **+39%** in 2021 to 2023 and only +7% after. Terrorism vocabulary **−12% / −9%** in topic text and −24% / −19% in leads (comparison flat); Hamas alone lost 47 mentions.
- **Confidence.** STRONG for genocide against comparison. SUGGESTIVE for the rest; the Nakba lexicon is contaminated (see data notes), the terrorism fall is a few articles, and colonial is mixed (D11).
- **Best chart.** Dumbbell per word family, % change 2021 to 2023 vs 2023 to 2026, topic vs comparison. `when_where_what/chart_data/07_vocabulary_families.json`. Back-up diverging bars: `when_where_what/chart_data/08_word_race.json`.
- **Example.** Hamas, 2021: "a Palestinian Sunni-Islamic fundamentalist but pragmatic, militant, and nationalist organization." 2026: "a Sunni Islamist Palestinian nationalist political organisation with a military wing known as the al-Qassam Brigades." The terrorist designations are still in the lead, lower down.
- **So what.** The post-war vocabulary is the vocabulary of courts and of 1948. Arguments that "apartheid entered Wikipedia after 7 October" are aimed at the wrong period. Changes also ran the other way: 'by Zionist militias' was cut from the 1948 expulsion first sentence, Israeli settlements now adds "but Israel disputes this", antisemitism appears in 54 topic leads instead of 37, and 'resistance movement' for Hamas did not stick. The picture is lopsided, not one-sided.

### P6. Other conflicts and other Wikipedias did not do this in the same way
*Merges HW1, HW3, WWW10, WWW11, SSL4, SSL5.*

- **Numbers.** At equal edit activity, topic articles gained **2 to 4x** as often as the pooled comparison (busiest tenth: 41% vs 19%). In the busiest fifth: Palestine 57%, Israel 44%, against **Ukraine 30%, Armenia 30%**, Bosnia 27%; Judaism 15%. Edit surges predict gains in topic articles (52% top quartile vs 31% bottom) and not in comparison articles. English leads: +0.24 per 1,000 words (p<0.001), Portuguese +0.27, the other 9 languages near zero (mean +0.06).
- **Confidence.** STRONG for topic vs pooled comparison. SUGGESTIVE for topic vs other live conflicts (D4) and for languages (D10).
- **Best chart.** Dot plot, share gaining by edit-activity decile, topic vs comparison. `how_who/chart_data/activity_decile_gain.json`. Then the project ranking `how_who/chart_data/busiest_by_project.json` and the language forest plot `story_sources_langs/chart_data/languages_lead_did.json`.
- **Example.** Tigray war went 0, 1, 8, 12, 19, 21 critical hits over its first six quarters and then stayed flat for four years, and the vocabulary did not spread to Ethiopian history or place pages. Across languages, 'genocide' entered the Gaza war lead in 9 of 11 editions but in different voices. Spanish: "ha desencadenado un genocidio en Gaza por parte de Israel"; Hebrew mentions only South Africa's ICJ case.
- **So what.** "Vocabulary follows wars" is normal Wikipedia behaviour. Two things set this topic apart: a given amount of editing turned into change more often, and the change left the war articles for history, places and people. The event label (genocide) spread across languages; the re-reading of history mostly stayed in English. That points to the English editing community and its processes, not to world events alone.

### P7. Quiet pages absorbed the change; the famous edit wars were on four pages and are now cooling
*Merges HW4, HW5, HW6, HW7, HW8, WWW9, WWW13. Holdout: revert moderation −3.9 [−6.8, −0.5] (exploratory). A8 ratchet did not replicate.*

- **Numbers.** Among busy mass-tier articles, **low-revert topic pages gained 28.5%** of the time, mid and high only 13.9% and 13.1% (comparison 10.5%, 10.3%, 6.5%). High-talk topic pages gained 60%, low-talk 22% (comparison flat). **Israel, Gaza war, Gaza genocide and Hamas hold 68%** of all lead flips in the 25 key articles. Lead flips per quarter: **129 in 2023-Q4, 7 to 13 in 2026**.
- **Confidence.** STRONG for "low-revert pages changed more" (mass n, comparison group, direction seen again in the holdout). SUGGESTIVE for talk-page deliberation (talk volume may be a result as well as a cause) and for cooling (2026 numbers are small and censored). ANECDOTE for the single-article histories.
- **Best chart.** Grouped bars, share gaining by revert tercile, topic vs comparison. `how_who/chart_data/revert_tercile_gain.json`. For the battleground story: `how_who/chart_data/flips_per_quarter.json` with `how_who/chart_data/archetypes.json`.
- **Example.** Gaza genocide lead, eight one-way steps in 21 months. 2024-01-01: "Allegations have been made that the 2023 Israel–Hamas war constituted a genocide against the Palestinians." 2025-01, edit summary: '"state" is more neutral than "accuse"'. 2025-10-05: "The Gaza genocide is the ongoing, intentional, and systematic destruction of the Palestinian people in the Gaza Strip carried out by Israel during the Gaza war." (summary: "per talk"). Against that, Israel's lead: 'apartheid' entered and left 39 times, each state lasting a median of about 12 hours.
- **So what.** The edit wars in the news are real, but they are a few pages. Most of the topic-specific change landed on pages where nobody reverted it, often after discussion. Anyone who monitors only edit wars misses most of it. On the battlegrounds a single edit rarely settles anything; terms that stuck took on average 3.3 failed tries by 3.4 editors. But insertions here survive less well than in comparison articles (36% vs 56%), so "push until it sticks" is a description of these pages, not a proven mechanism (D5).

### P8. It was written by an established crowd of regulars, not by a flood of newcomers, and the timing does not look scheduled
*Merges HW9, HW10, HW11, FINDINGS co-editing.*

- **Numbers.** Edits by accounts new that quarter: **41% in 2023-Q4**, then **12 to 17%** from late 2024 (about 25% before the war; comparison 25 to 34% throughout). Anonymous edits 17% to 5%. **Eight editors** changed first sentences on 3 or more key articles, making 22% of 208 lasting changes (comparison: none). Overlap among the 150 most active topic editors rose from 1.11x to **1.61x** a popularity null (comparison 1.17x to 1.08x). Zero editor pairs edited within 30 minutes of each other on 5 or more articles.
- **Confidence.** SUGGESTIVE. The link to the extended-confirmed restriction is inferred, since we have no protection logs; editor hashes cannot be linked across files; and the sweep figures conflict between tiers (D7).
- **Best chart.** Line chart by quarter, newcomer and anonymous edit share, topic vs comparison. `how_who/chart_data/who_edits_by_quarter.json`. Secondary dot plot: `how_who/chart_data/coordination_signals.json`.
- **Example.** Israeli apartheid, 2024-08-05, a topic regular's edit summary: "taking the first step in summarizing the body in the lede to reflect the article's new scope". The first sentence became "Israeli apartheid refers to a system of systematic discrimination...".
- **So what.** "Brigading by new accounts" is not what the data show. The text was written by a small, overlapping community of experienced regulars, which fits the topic's extended-confirmed rules. That is consistent with an interest-based community watching the same pages. These signals cannot detect off-wiki coordination or sockpuppets, and they cannot rule them out.

### P9. The words changed more than the sources did, and most new words arrived attributed
*Merges SSL8, SSL13, SSL2, SSL9, WWW12, holdout B4 and K01.*

- **Numbers.** Mass-tier topic citations: Arab and pro-Palestinian outlets **0.63% to 1.68%**, pro-Israel advocacy 0.95% to 0.57%, Israeli press steady at about 7%, rights groups and UN steady at about 1.5%. The scholarly share rose 37% to 54%, but just as much in comparison articles (48% to 63%). Attribution share of loaded terms, mass: topic **21% to 25%**, comparison 17% flat. Deep leads: 0.31 (2021), 0.43 (Jan 2024), **0.26 (2026)**.
- **Confidence.** STRONG for sources (large n, comparison, consistent across tiers). SUGGESTIVE for attribution, because the tiers diverge (D1). ANECDOTE for the individual voice upgrades.
- **Best chart.** Small multiples, citation share by source class, topic and comparison, scholarly in grey. `story_sources_langs/chart_data/citations.json`. Second: two-panel hedging line, mass vs deep leads. `story_sources_langs/chart_data/hedging.json`.
- **Example.** Israel lead, 2026: "After the Hamas-led October 7 attacks, Israel began committing genocide against Palestinians in Gaza. Israel and its allies, including the United States, deny that Israel's actions constitute genocide." Contrast, Uyghur genocide, 2026: "... which has often been characterized as persecution or as genocide."
- **So what.** The reframing did not come from a flood of partisan sources. Most of the new critical wording came in as reported claims. The step that matters to readers is the attribution disappearing, and that happened in a small number of high-traffic leads. Whether that is unusual needs an atrocity comparison set (Uyghur, Rohingya, Darfur, Yazidi, Bosnia), which is being collected. ADL citations fell in 2021-22 and were flat through the June 2024 reliability ruling, so source rulings may record what editors already did rather than trigger a purge.

## What we can conclude

Said at the right strength.

**We can say (strong):**
- After October 2023, more English Wikipedia articles on Israel and Palestine gained critical vocabulary than comparison articles did. It replicated on a sealed half of the data (+3.6 pp).
- The change is concentrated: in Israel and Palestine projects, in leads and first sentences, and in a few hundred pages, including the most-read ones.
- Articles on Judaism did not change (formally equivalent to zero).
- It came in 2024 and has been flat across the article mass since early 2025.
- It came as added text, mostly about older history. Jewish history content was kept.
- The source mix barely changed beyond a Wikipedia-wide rise in scholarly citations.
- Pages with few reverts changed more than contested pages.

**We can say (suggestive):**
- Editing turned into change more often here than in other live conflicts, and the change spread beyond war articles, which other conflicts' waves did not.
- English (and Portuguese) leads moved; most other language editions did not detectably.
- The writers were mostly established regulars, with fewer newcomers than before the war.
- New labels mostly came in attributed. A few flagship leads later dropped the attribution.
- When a rhetorical move happens in a topic article, it usually points one way, but such moves are not more frequent than in comparison articles, and the counts are small.

**We cannot conclude:**
- **Truth or accuracy.** Counting words measures emphasis. "Genocide" can appear more because a court case exists. The study does not rule on who is right.
- **Intent.** We cannot see why editors wrote what they wrote.
- **Coordination.** Co-editing and overlap patterns also come from shared watchlists and breaking news. We found no synchronized tag-teaming, but we cannot detect or rule out off-wiki coordination or sockpuppets.
- **That nothing happened.** The pre-registered deep-panel average test was null and underpowered for leads. "Not supported" means "not shown".
- **Who a word is aimed at.** A 'genocide' or 'expulsion' hit can be about Jews, Palestinians or others (see the Nakba lexicon note).
- **Causes.** Protection rules, talk-page processes and editor composition all fit the pattern. We have not separated them.
- **Effect on readers.** We know what text was live when people visited. We do not know what they took from it.

## What someone could do with this

**Readers**
- Read the lead of a contested article as the current state of a negotiation. Open "View history" and the talk page before quoting a first sentence.
- Check whether a loaded label is attributed ("according to", "X state that") or in Wikipedia's voice. The difference is often a single recent edit.
- For contested history, read the body. The body keeps the competing views even when the first sentence does not.

**Wikipedia editors and the community**
- Audit the low-revert pages in the Israel and Palestine projects (villages, places, biographies), not only the battlegrounds. That is where most of the uncontested change landed.
- Make voice changes visible. A move from attributed claim to Wikipedia's voice on a high-traffic lead could get a talk-page note or an RfC by default, as page moves already do.
- Compare like with like. Apply the same voice standard to Gaza, Uyghur, Rohingya and other contested atrocity articles, and document where they differ and why.
- Check that short descriptions and Wikidata descriptions match the article consensus. Fewer editors watch them, and they reach more people.

**Journalists and researchers**
- Date claims properly. The vocabulary step was in 2024, apartheid language came before the war, and the ADL ruling did not trigger a visible drop.
- Weight by pageviews. An article-count sample will under-state what readers saw, and a battleground-only study will miss the quiet majority of changes.
- Use voice level (alleged, accused, "X states", own voice) as an outcome, not just term counts.
- Always report a comparison group. Several "trends" (scholarly citations, 'colonial', stripping attribution) are Wikipedia-wide.
- Go to talk archives and RfC closes. Edit summaries rarely say why (7% cite talk or consensus).

**Platforms that reuse Wikipedia (search, AI assistants, knowledge panels)**
- Treat the first sentence of a contested-topic article as volatile. Israel's lead wording settled only on 2026-09-04, after 19 genocide flips and 39 apartheid flips.
- Carry attribution through. If the source sentence says "X state that", the snippet or answer should too. Flag where Wikipedia itself has moved a label into its own voice.
- Snapshot carefully. A lead scraped in 2024 can differ from the 2026 consensus. Record the revision ID used.
- Monitor the reuse surfaces: short description, Wikidata description, first sentence.

**The owner's own monitoring**
- **"Watch the hot core."** About 50 articles chosen by pageviews × lead changes. The 60 deep-panel lead gainers already carry 46% of topic views. Start from Israel, Benjamin Netanyahu, Gaza Strip, Israeli–Palestinian conflict, Zionism, Jerusalem, Jews, Gaza war, Palestine, Israel Defense Forces, Hamas, Nakba, Gaza genocide, Israeli apartheid, West Bank, plus the top mass gainers.
- Weekly: diff the first sentence and lead; record term presence, voice level, short description and Wikidata description. Alert on a voice-level change, a new term that survives 7 days, or a short-description change.
- Monthly: refresh ratchet.json for the 40 key articles to test whether the 2026 ebb (29 to 26 pairs) is a counter-wave (WWW Q8).
- Quarterly: rerun the mass-tier gain share with the comparison group and the Judaism equivalence test, so the "flat since 2025" claim stays current.
- Keep a matched comparison watchlist (Ukraine, Armenia, Uyghur, Sudan, Tigray) so every alert has a baseline.

## Data-quality notes to fix

1. **125 duplicate redirect rows in mass.** `mass_results.json` lab_articles has 125 rows where redirects resolve to the same page (Palestine / Palestinian state / Palestinian homeland; West Bank / Economy of the West Bank). WWW deduplicated (9,062 unique articles with three snapshots); HW used 9,175 and may not have. The FINDINGS and holdout mass numbers predate the fix and may be slightly inflated. Fix: dedupe by resolved page ID in the pipeline and rerun the mass figures, including A1.
2. **The Nakba/expulsion lexicon catches Jewish expulsions.** 76 of 410 net added hits are in Judaism and Jewish-history articles (Edict of Expulsion +20). Fix: split the lexicon into Palestinian-1948 and other expulsion senses, or require a 1948/Palestinian co-term in the window; rerun A3 and the vocabulary families.
3. **Pageviews counted by current title.** Views under former titles are lost ('Israel and the apartheid analogy', '2023 Israel–Hamas war', Gaza genocide's earlier titles). This understates early exposure and biases SSL3's "views before the wording settled". Fix: pull move logs and redirects and sum views across all titles.
4. **Title-keyword article-type classifier at about 70% accuracy.** Untyped titles (mostly places) are a large residual. Fix: classify with Wikidata P31 (instance of) or WikiProject infobox types, and hand-check a sample of 200.
5. **Top-decile concentration is partly mechanical.** "Top 10% supplied 96%" mostly restates that only 11.4% of articles gained. Lead with the 1% (38%) and 5% (78%) figures.
6. **Per-article mean vs pooled rate.** WWW1's pooled rate per 10k words is weighted toward long articles and uses 84 "quiet" comparison articles (excluding 6 shocked). The pre-registered P2 used per-article means and all 90. Always say which. This is the arithmetic behind "the average didn't move".
7. **Sweep metric conflict.** Deep-sample and mass-tier sweep rates point in opposite directions (D7). Use one definition and report both tiers.
8. **Hedge regex.** The 120/60-character window counts denials and quotations as hedges. Validate on 100 hand-coded mentions before quoting attribution shares.
9. **Ratchet counts.** Reconcile "35 terms stuck" with "25 gained, 6 lost" (D12), and the Zionism 44 cycles vs 105 flips. Document which feature pattern each uses.
10. **Censoring in survival by year.** 2026 insertions have had less time to fail; HW6's 77% (n=13) should use a Kaplan-Meier estimate, not a raw share.
11. **Editor hashes salted per run.** Editors cannot be linked across files, which blocks cross-file who-did-what analysis. Use one fixed secret salt for the pilot and the mass rerun, then discard it at the end.
12. **Language comparisons.** English uses a 7-term lexicon on all articles; other languages use 5 translated stems on about 550 top articles. Compare signs and rough sizes only, or rerun English on the same top-550 design.
13. **Source classes.** 'Arab & pro-Palestinian' mixes Al Jazeera, +972 (Israeli) and Anadolu (Turkish), and 25 to 45% of citations are 'Other'. Refine before quoting the 11.7:1 to 4.2:1 ratio as a press-balance measure.
14. **Cross-references in how_who/cards.md are off by one** (card 4 cites "card 7" for low-revert, which is card 8; Q1 cites "card 8" for newcomers, which is card 9). Fix before sharing the cards.
15. **Deep-panel gaps.** About 100 articles miss 1 to 3 quarters and were forward-filled. Flag them in quarterly charts.
