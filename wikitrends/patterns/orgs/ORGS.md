# Organised efforts around English Wikipedia's Israel/Palestine coverage, 2008–2026: the public record

**Scope.** This file covers organisations and publicly documented initiatives, on every side. It does not identify pseudonymous editors: ArbCom-sanctioned accounts are counted, not named, even where the press names them. Individuals are named only where they acted publicly for an organisation.

**Sourcing.**
- Every item was read as a search-engine **SNIPPET**. Page fetches failed in this session: en.wikipedia.org and the mirrors were unreachable.
- Nothing here is FULLTEXT. Every line needs checking against the primary page before publication.
- Outlets with a stated position are labelled: (pro-Isr) pro-Israel advocacy or commentary; (pro-Pal) pro-Palestinian; (ArbCom/WMF) Wikipedia's own bodies.

**Tests.** Testable footprints are in `footprints.json`. The tests we ran are in `tests.md`; none found a footprint distinguishable from chance.

## 3. What Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee found (general terms)

- **2008.** Admins linked list members' accounts and sanctioned five. ArbCom later tightened conduct rules for the area.
- **Standing regime.** The topic is a contentious topic under the extended-confirmed restriction (ARBPIA4, 2019; amended 2022). Only accounts with 500 edits and 30 days' tenure may edit.
- **Jan 2024, "PIA canvassing" motions.** ArbCom found **email canvassing** in the topic area aimed at promoting a **pro-Israel** viewpoint, and **proxy editing on behalf of a banned editor**, dating from about Oct 2023. Two editors were found most likely to have taken part, on CheckUser and private evidence. Six motions passed.
- **Nov 2024, case request "Covert canvassing and proxying in the Israel-Arab conflict topic area".** It alleged off-wiki coordination promoting a **pro-Palestinian** viewpoint through a **Discord group and an "EEML-style mailing list"**.
  - The mailing-list part is an **allegation in the request**.
  - We found **no snippet showing that ArbCom found a mailing list**. Verify this before publishing.
- **Dec 2024, private case "Off-wiki misconduct in Palestine–Israel topic area".** ArbCom sanctioned editors for **editing after off-wiki canvassing requests** on the TfP Discord channel and for "encouraging other users to game the extended confirmed restriction". One was site-banned and one topic-banned.
- **23 Jan 2025, PIA5.** About 8 indefinite topic bans, reported as 6 "pro-Palestinian" and 2 "pro-Israel", plus a **balanced editing restriction**.
  - The grounds were conduct (battleground editing), **not a coordination finding**.
  - One veteran admin was warned. Counts differ by outlet.
- **25 Apr 2025.** ArbCom site-banned two more editors on "**additional evidence of off-wiki coordination independent of the Tech4Palestine Discord server**". The dossier's concerns were "adequately addressed", and the evidence was kept for corroboration.
- **Early 2026.** A PIA5-sanctioned veteran was site-banned. Pirate Wires reported it on 20 Mar 2026; the vote is reported as 7–5 or as unanimous, and the conflict is unresolved.

**In short.** ArbCom has made **off-wiki coordination findings on both sides**: pro-Israel email canvassing and proxying (Jan 2024), and pro-Palestinian Discord canvassing plus further off-wiki coordination (Dec 2024 and Apr 2025). It has not adopted the press figures for the size of either group. Its broad remedy (PIA5) was framed as conduct-based and hit both camps.

## 4. Documented vs alleged vs disproven

**Documented** (a primary record, or the organisation's own statement):
- CAMERA's 2008 list and its sanctions.
- The Yesha Council / My Israel course in 2010 (~50 people).
- The Euro-Med WikiRights rounds (2021, 2026; its own announcements).
- Arts and academic edit-a-thons on Palestine (2024–25; organisers' pages).
- The Toronto edit-a-thon announcement.
- The existence of the TfP Wikipedia channel, and ArbCom sanctions for off-wiki canvassing tied to it.
- ArbCom's Jan 2024 finding of pro-Israel email canvassing and proxying.
- The PIA5 sanctions on both sides.
- The Heritage plan, as a leaked deck.
- The Israel Group's announced program.
- The Zionism petition.
- The NGO Monitor staffer's 2013 sanction.

**Alleged** (reported, disputed or unverified):
- That about 40 editors (Pirate Wires) or about 30 (ADL) acted as one coordinated group, and that this "hijacked" or "reshaped" coverage. The WMF called the ADL's conclusions flawed. ArbCom sanctioned individuals, not a 40-person group.
- Scale figures for TfP: "100+" articles, "thousands" of articles.
- ADL's claim of a 95% decline (2026), and its claim of "8 original + 11 new" accounts.
- That Euro-Med is "Hamas-linked" (NGO Monitor and Israeli officials; disputed).
- That the Toronto event produced edits to the Azrieli pages (JPost).
- That the French club intended to manipulate Wikipedia (its co-founder denies it).
- The impact claims on both sides: Wales on 2010, "absolutely no impact"; the 2008 admins, "little effect".

**Disproven, contradicted or likely errors:**
- **"8,000 articles"** appears to be the TfP Discord's ~8,000 **members**. Sources conflict; treat it as a likely conflation until the Pirate Wires text is checked.
- **Timing.** In our own data, the wave of critical-term insertions in topic leads came *before* the TfP channel existed. The topic/comparison ratio was 3.4× from Oct 2023 to Jan 2024 and 2.0× while the channel was active. A campaign that began in Jan 2024 cannot explain the war-onset surge. This contradicts any claim that the channel *started* the shift. It does not test whether the channel added to it. See `tests.md`.
- **Government-directed editing in 2021–2026.** We found no reputable report. The 2025 Israeli contracts concern web and AI content, not Wikipedia editing, and must not be reported as Wikipedia operations.
