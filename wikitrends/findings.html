What we found

The main test came back negative. Before collecting any data, we wrote down the test and the rule for calling the result. The test: did words like "genocide", "apartheid", "occupation" and "colonial" grow more in 422 articles about Israel, Jews and Zionism than in articles about other countries and conflicts? By that rule, the answer is not supported. The full text of the articles shows no extra change. The opening paragraphs might have changed, but the test was not strong enough to tell either way.

In a far bigger sample, a small change shows up, and only after October 2023. Across 10,433 articles, conflict vocabulary grew slightly more in the topic articles after the war began. That works out to about one extra such word per 20,000 words of text. The rise is concentrated in articles tagged Palestine and Israel. Articles about Judaism did not change at all. Articles about other wars (Armenia, Russia, Ukraine) rose by similar amounts.

English Wikipedia is not an outlier. The same articles in German, French, Hebrew, Arabic and six other languages changed by similar amounts.

The editing pattern looks different, but not one-sided. The same pairs of editors repeatedly edit right after each other on these articles 2–3 times more often per edit than on comparison articles. After the war, the most active editors' sets of articles also overlapped more. Shared watchlists and breaking news produce exactly this pattern, so it is a signal, not proof of coordination. Words did not "slip in and stay" more often here than elsewhere. Topic articles change more often, but they are undone less often.

When opening paragraphs did change direction, the changes often stated contested claims as fact. One example: "Israeli apartheid" moved from presenting apartheid as a contested analogy to stating it as fact. This happened in both directions and in comparison articles too. The overall difference between groups was not significant. AI readers did this coding. They could not be fully blinded, because the AI recognised the masked articles 25 times out of 30.

What this cannot tell you. Counting words shows emphasis, not truth. It cannot show whether a term is aimed at Israel or at others. Editing patterns are not proof of intent. "Not supported" means "not shown", not "nothing happened".
