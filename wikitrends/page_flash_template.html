Israel on Wikipedia, 2021–2026Full evidence →

Before the war, no charged Israel with genocide. Now the article on Israel does.

This report measures how the wording of the core articles people read changed, not whether it is true, and does not assess the conduct of the war or of any government.

1 ZionismFull evidence →

Zionism was the “re-establishment” of a Jewish state. Now it is “a Jewish homeland through colonization.”

◀ Drag through time ▶Marked: “re-establishment” and “state” (out), “colonial” or “colonization” (in). Other words changed too; the button shows them all.

Of the 41 removals of “colonization” we can check, 37 were undone.

The same kind of change reached 1948.

2 1948 and the NakbaFull evidence →

The Nakba was “the destruction of Palestinian society.” Now it is “the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists.”

Three 1948 articles now name, in Wikipedia’s own voice, who drove the Palestinians out: “Zionists” or “Zionist forces.” For the Jews who “fled or were expelled from Arab states,” no one is named.

Both full sentences
Nakba · Dec 2021 and since 6 Jul 2026 · the 2021 version also took a positionThen “The Nakba, also known as the Palestinian Catastrophe, was the destruction of Palestinian society and homeland in 1948, and the permanent displacement of a majority of the Palestinian Arabs.”Now “The Nakba (النَّكْبَة) is the ethnic cleansing of Palestinian Arabs by Zionists through their violent displacement and dispossession of land, property, and belongings …”

And it reached October 7.

3 October 7 and the hostagesFull evidence →

Wikipedia’s first line on October 7 now says “coordinated armed incursions.” In 107 versions, it has never mentioned the hostages.

What changed · Oct 2023 / Jan 2024 → now“A series of coordinated attacks”→ became “a series of coordinated armed incursions”“rape and sexual assault by Hamas fighters”→ became “dozens of cases of rape and sexual assault.”

The opening still says “massacring civilians in 21 communities,” and the first sentence names Hamas, later on.

The group that carried out the attack got a new definition too.

4 Hamas, English and HebrewEvidence →Languages →

In 2021 Wikipedia called Hamas “militant.” Now it is a “political organisation with a military wing.”

EnglishHamas · first sentence · since 29 Nov 2025“… is a Sunni Islamist Palestinian nationalist political organisation with a military wing known as the al-Qassam Brigades.”
עברית · HebrewExploratoryHebrew Wikipedia · now (Oct 2026)“חַמַאס (בערבית: حماس) הוא ארגון טרור פלסטיני אסלאמיסטי-סוני הפועל גם כתנועה פוליטית.”“Hamas (in Arabic: حماس) is a Palestinian Sunni-Islamist terror organisation that also operates as a political movement.”

Hebrew Wikipedia puts it the other way around, as it already did in 2021. In English, “militant” left on 12 October 2023, five days after the attack. It came back three times, last in February 2025.

Context: in line with Wikipedia-wide style on contentious labels, “terrorist” left the first sentences of Islamic State, al-Shabaab and Boko Haram. Hamas’s first sentence did not have it in 2021.

How did these lines get there? In four first sentences, the history shows which wording stayed and which was taken out.

6 How it got thereEvidence →Timing →

Since September 2025, 10 edits tried to make the Gaza genocide sentence attributed again. Each was gone within 45 minutes.

How long a word lasts
Gaza genocide · first sentence · Dec 2023 → now

Own voice since 26 September 2025. 10 edits tried to bring back attribution (“alleged,” “described by most scholars as”); each was gone within 45 minutes.

Hamas · first sentence · 2021 → now

“Terrorist” went in five times; the longest stay was about 2.4 hours. The words “resistance movement” got in once, on 1 August 2024, and were gone the next day.

Zionism · first sentence · 2021 → now

“Colonial” was taken out and put back 10 times from November 2024 to August 2026; each removal was undone within a day.

These are four first sentences (October 7 is in card 3), not the whole subject. Across the 254 core, Top- and High-importance topic articles, reverts of edits that removed critical terms came no faster than reverts of edits that added them.

7 From Wikipedia’s arbitratorsFull evidence →

Wikipedia’s arbitrators found outside actors “heavily invested in influencing” its coverage of this topic. They named no side or country.

Arbitration Committee · PIA5 · finding 10 · adopted 15–0 · 23 Jan 2025“Outside actors are heavily invested in influencing Wikipedia's coverage of the topic area”It also recorded that the account Ïvana was banned “for off-wiki misconduct relating to the PIA topic area” (finding 16, 9 Dec 2024).

Our count: in 217 formal talk-page discussions, four groups of 3 to 6 accounts voted together 89 to 100% of the time, against about half by chance.

That is camp voting, not proof of coordination; we did not code which side each group took, nor tie either finding to the edits above.

In shortBefore the war, none of the most-read Wikipedia openings on the subject charged Israel with genocide; now the article on Israel says, in Wikipedia’s voice, that Israel “began committing genocide,” Zionism is “a Jewish homeland through colonization,” and, in every copy we hold, the Israel and Hamas opening sections do not mention the hostages.
